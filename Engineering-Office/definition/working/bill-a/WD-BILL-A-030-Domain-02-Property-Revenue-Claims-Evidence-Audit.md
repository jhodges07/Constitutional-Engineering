## A. Executive Domain 02 finding

**WHAT PROPERTY-BASED COMPULSORY GOVERNMENTAL CLAIMS CURRENTLY EXIST IN KANSAS?**

Kansas currently imposes a **classified ad valorem property-tax system** on taxable real property and taxable tangible personal property, plus **in-lieu / formula property-based vehicle taxes**, plus **specified statewide mills**, plus **variable local mill levies** by counties, cities, unified school districts, townships, and other taxing subdivisions.

The core legal trigger of the general property tax is **taxable tangible property being classified, valued, assessed, and placed on the tax roll** as of the assessment architecture in K.S.A. Chapter 79 — not a retail consumption event.

This is a **current-state existence** answer from PRIMARY-LEGAL / GOV-DATA. It is **not** a Bill A RETAIN, TRANSFORM, or DISAPPEAR decision. It is **not** a conclusion that the Constitution requires perpetual retention of the current system, and **not** a conclusion that elimination is constitutionally permitted.

### 2. Which levels / types of government impose them?

**State:** 20-mill school levy is levied by each USD but remitted to the **state school district finance fund** (72-5142(c)); statewide educational-building and institutions-building mills **in tax year 2025** (76-6b01 / 76-6b04). Beginning tax year **2026**, those statewide mills are **discontinued** (L. 2025, ch. 71 / SB 35; KLRD summary). 76-6b02(c) / 76-6b05(e) begin July 1, 2026 **SGF transfers** — those transfers are **not** Domain 02 mills (**REFERRED TO DOMAIN 09**). Do not carry the 2025 mill into 2026.  
**County / city / township / USD / other taxing subdivisions:** certify levies to the county clerk (79-1801); county treasurer collects and distributes.

### 3. What property classes are subject?

Kansas Constitution Art. 11 §1 (on and after Jan. 1, 2013) and K.S.A. 79-1439: **Class 1 real property** (seven subclasses) and **Class 2 tangible personal property** (six subclasses), at constitutional/statutory assessment percentages. Motor vehicles under the 79-5101 act, recreational vehicles under 79-5118 et seq., and (through Dec. 31, 2025) watercraft under 79-5501 are taxed **in lieu** or by special formula. **On and after Jan. 1, 2026, all watercraft are exempt** (79-5501(e)).

### 4. How does valuation / assessment architecture work?

```text
PROPERTY
→ CLASSIFICATION (Art. 11 §1 / 79-1439 subclass)
→ APPRAISED VALUE
     real (non-ag): fair market value (79-503a / 79-1476(b))
     agricultural land: use/productivity value (Art. 11 §12 / 79-1476(c))
     CIME personal: retail-cost-new less depreciation, floor 20% (Art. 11 §1 / 79-1439)
→ ASSESSED VALUE = appraised × subclass percentage
→ MILL LEVY (certified under 79-1801; 1 mill = $1 per $1,000 assessed)
→ TAX LIABILITY
```

Do **not** collapse agricultural use value, residential 11.5%, commercial 25%, and public-utility 33% into one generic formula.

### 5. How are mill levies / rates applied?

Each taxing subdivision certifies the **amount** of ad valorem tax to be levied on or before October 1 (79-1801, as amended L. 2025, ch. 128). The county clerk places the tax on the roll; the county treasurer collects. Actual combined mill rates are **VARIABLE BY JURISDICTION**. PVD Table IV 2025 statewide **average county levy 127.035 mills** (range includes Coffey County average 93.873 to Greeley 270.739).

### 13. Ownership / existence versus consumption event

**From evidence, without converting the finding into a Bill A legal or political conclusion:**

Kansas general ad valorem taxation attaches to **taxable tangible property classified and valued for the tax year**, typically as of the assessment date architecture in Chapter 79, because the property **exists, is owned or possesses tax situs in the jurisdiction, is classified, and is assessed**. Motor-vehicle and recreational-vehicle taxes similarly attach to the **vehicle as property** (registration is the collection event, not a FairTax consumption event). That structure is **STRUCTURALLY OUTSIDE the H.R. 25 consumption taxable event** and is a **POTENTIAL CONFLICT WITH BILL A HUMAN INTENT** that mere existence/ownership of property is not the intended Kansas FairTax taxable event. **Field 25 remains BLANK.**

# BILL A — HUMAN ENGINEERING INTENT
WORKING DEFINITION — FUNDAMENTAL PROBLEM AND STRUCTURAL OBJECTIVES

## 1. PROPERTY RIGHTS PRECEDE GOVERNMENT POOLING

Bill A shall begin with the AGCL principle that:

PROPERTY RIGHTS PRECEDE GOVERNMENT POOLING.

Government's authority to impose taxation does not create the individual's underlying property right.

The starting point of the tax architecture is therefore the person and that person's property — not the government revenue pool.

Government must establish legitimate authority before private wealth is transferred into a government-controlled pool.

## 2. CLEAR SIGNALS BEFORE GOVERNMENT REVENUE CLAIMS

Government must provide CLEAR SIGNALS before making a revenue claim against the taxpayer.

The architecture must define, at minimum:

the authority for the claim;

the taxable event;

who becomes obligated;

when the obligation occurs;

how the obligation is determined;

where the collected money is authorized to go;

the limits of the authority;

and when that authority expires or must be reconsidered.

Government should not be permitted to establish an ambiguous revenue claim first and explain or broaden its authority afterward.

SIGNAL MUST PRECEDE CLAIM.

## 3. THERE IS ONLY ONE ULTIMATE SOURCE OF TAXATION: PEOPLE WITH MONEY

Bill A begins from a basic economic principle:

THERE IS ONLY ONE ULTIMATE SOURCE OF TAXATION:

PEOPLE WITH MONEY.

Period.

Government can create many different tax names, collection mechanisms, legal classifications, accounting categories, and points of collection.

That does not create additional ultimate sources of taxation.

A corporation does not create money independently of people.

A business does not create a second class of taxpayer separate from people.

A retailer collecting a tax does not create another source of tax revenue.

An employer withholding a tax does not create another source of tax revenue.

A property owner, consumer, worker, investor, customer, shareholder, business owner, lender, borrower, or beneficiary may encounter the tax system through different legal or economic relationships.

Ultimately, however, the economic resources come from people.

The system may change: WHO COLLECTS; WHEN COLLECTION OCCURS; WHAT TRANSACTION IS USED; WHAT ACCOUNT RECORDS THE PAYMENT; WHAT LEGAL ENTITY TRANSMITS THE PAYMENT; or WHAT GOVERNMENT CALLS THE TAX.

None of those changes creates another ultimate source of taxation.

SHUFFLING THE DECK DOES NOT CREATE ANOTHER TAX SOURCE.

## 4. TAX COMPLEXITY MUST NOT HIDE THIS REALITY

Bill A shall investigate the proposition that maintaining a plethora of taxes, fees, assessments, levies, surcharges, classifications, withholding mechanisms, and revenue schemes can obscure the basic economic reality that government ultimately obtains its tax resources from people.

Complexity can make it difficult for the taxpayer to determine:

how much government actually costs;

how much tax the individual ultimately bears;

where taxation occurs;

who merely collects or transmits the tax;

what event creates the obligation;

where the money goes;

and what governmental purpose claims the money.

Bill A should therefore distinguish between:

THE ULTIMATE SOURCE OF TAXATION

and

THE MECHANISM USED TO COLLECT OR TRANSMIT TAXATION.

Creating another collection mechanism does not create another source of government revenue.

The objective is not merely to reduce the number of tax names.

The objective is to make the underlying economic reality visible.

## 5. TAXPAYER OWNERSHIP AND GOVERNMENT STEWARDSHIP

A central Bill A objective shall be:

TAXPAYER OWNERSHIP OF TAX DOLLARS

AND

GOVERNMENT STEWARDSHIP OF TAX DOLLARS.

Bill A shall investigate an architecture under which payment into a government pool does not automatically mean that government should be treated as having an unlimited ownership claim over the collected resources.

Government should instead exercise bounded stewardship authority over taxpayer resources for authorized governmental purposes.

The engineering process must determine what taxpayer ownership means:

before collection;

at the taxable event;

during collection;

after pooling;

during expenditure;

when authorized spending does not occur;

when authority expires;

and when a government node fails.

The legal feasibility and constitutional consequences of continuing taxpayer ownership after payment remain questions requiring independent legal research.

## 6. CONSTITUTIONALLY AUTHORIZED SPENDING

Bill A cannot engineer the revenue side of government without addressing the expenditure side.

The amount government currently spends SHALL NOT automatically define the amount of revenue Bill A must replace.

Existing expenditure is not automatically accepted as the legitimate future revenue requirement.

Bill A must therefore investigate a controlled method for auditing and classifying government spending at both:

STATE LEVEL

and

LOCAL LEVEL.

The purpose is to determine what spending is constitutionally authorized and what authority supports it.

Existing programs, appropriations, agencies, contracts, or historical spending patterns shall not automatically establish permanent claims against taxpayers merely because they currently exist.

The precise constitutional boundaries of state and local spending must be researched and verified rather than assumed.

## 7. REVENUE REPLACEMENT MUST NOT ASSUME CURRENT SPENDING

Bill A shall reject the automatic equation:

CURRENT TAX REVENUE = REQUIRED REPLACEMENT REVENUE.

Before determining how much replacement revenue is necessary, the engineering process must examine:

authorized governmental functions;

constitutional obligations;

existing lawful debt;

contractual obligations;

transition obligations;

government performance;

government efficiency;

and expenditures that may lack continuing authority.

The engineering question is therefore not merely:

"How do we replace every dollar government collects today?"

The question is:

"What revenue is actually required after legitimate governmental obligations, constitutional authority, performance, and stewardship are examined?"

## 8. SINKING FUNDS AND GOVERNMENT FINANCIAL PLANNING

Bill A shall investigate requiring government to use sinking funds and other controlled reserves as fundamental financial-management tools.

Government should be expected to plan for:

known future expenditures;

capital replacement;

economic downturns;

maintenance;

long-duration obligations;

transition requirements;

and foreseeable financial risks.

The taxpayer should not automatically serve as the financial backstop for government's failure to plan for predictable costs.

A government that knows an expense is coming should be expected to prepare financially for that expense.

## 9. TAX RATES SHALL NOT BE TREATED AS NATURAL CONSTANTS

Bill A rejects the assumption that tax rates should be treated as permanent or naturally constant.

Treating a tax rate as an effectively permanent entitlement can weaken incentives for:

government savings;

sinking-fund accumulation;

productivity improvement;

cost reduction;

better planning;

better purchasing;

better capital management;

and timely decisions.

Bill A shall investigate a system in which government understands that future revenue authority cannot simply be presumed.

The architecture should create continuing pressure to:

SAVE MONEY;

PLAN AHEAD;

IMPROVE PERFORMANCE;

REDUCE UNNECESSARY COST;

and

MAKE DECISIONS WITH URGENCY.

Tax authority should not become an automatic substitute for financial discipline.

## 10. TAXPAYERS SHALL NOT BE THE AUTOMATIC BACKSTOP

The days of treating taxpayers as the automatic backstop for:

bad planning;

poor performance;

failure to save;

failure to maintain assets;

failure to prepare for predictable obligations;

and ineffective governmental operations

should end.

When a government organization performs poorly, the first engineering response should not automatically be:

RAISE MORE REVENUE.

The architecture should first make visible:

what failed;

why it failed;

who was responsible;

what resources were provided;

what performance was expected;

whether the authority remains justified;

and what corrective action is required.

## 11. FOUR-MONTH NODE ACCOUNTABILITY

Bill A shall investigate a NODE-BASED ACCOUNTABILITY SYSTEM operating on a four-month accountability cycle.

Government spending and authorized functions should be represented by defined governmental nodes capable of being evaluated against their authorized purpose and measurable obligations.

At least once every four months, each applicable node should be required to demonstrate that it continues to satisfy the objects and conditions supporting its authority.

The four-month review should investigate, as applicable:

AUTHORITY;

PURPOSE;

PERFORMANCE;

FINANCIAL CONDITION;

RESOURCE USE;

SINKING-FUND CONDITION;

REQUIRED SIGNALS;

TRANSPARENCY;

OUTCOMES;

and

CONTINUING JUSTIFICATION.

The exact definition of a node, applicable measurement standard, failure threshold, corrective process, and legal consequences remain to be engineered.

## 12. NODE FAILURE MUST HAVE CONSEQUENCES

Accountability without consequences is only reporting.

Bill A shall therefore investigate what happens when a government node fails its controlled accountability requirements.

Possible consequences must be engineered rather than presumed and may involve questions concerning:

continued spending authority;

corrective-action periods;

management intervention;

competitive replacement;

reauthorization;

expiration;

classification of spending authority;

return or redirection of unneeded resources;

and taxpayer rights.

No specific failure consequence is accepted at this Definition stage.

The principle being established is:

FAILURE MUST PRODUCE A CONTROLLED SIGNAL AND A CONTROLLED RESPONSE.

## 13. GOVERNMENT REVENUE AUTHORITY MUST CREATE DISCIPLINE

Bill A should be designed so that revenue architecture itself creates incentives for responsible government behavior.

The system should discourage the assumption:

"IF GOVERNMENT NEEDS MORE MONEY, TAXPAYERS WILL PROVIDE IT."

Instead, government should operate knowing that:

authority is bounded;

signals are required;

performance is measured;

resources must be stewarded;

future obligations must be planned for;

sinking funds must be considered;

nodes are periodically evaluated;

and continuing revenue authority cannot simply be presumed.

## 14. STRUCTURAL DEFINITION OF SUCCESS

Bill A will be considered structurally successful only if the replacement architecture can establish and protect the following objectives:

PROPERTY RIGHTS BEFORE GOVERNMENT POOLING;

CLEAR SIGNALS BEFORE REVENUE CLAIMS;

ONE ULTIMATE SOURCE OF TAXATION — PEOPLE WITH MONEY;

CLEAR SEPARATION BETWEEN TAX SOURCE AND TAX COLLECTION MECHANISM;

VISIBLE TAXPAYER BURDEN;

EXPLICITLY DEFINED TAXABLE EVENTS;

BOUNDED GOVERNMENT REVENUE AUTHORITY;

TAXPAYER OWNERSHIP AS AN ENGINEERING OBJECTIVE;

GOVERNMENT STEWARDSHIP RATHER THAN UNLIMITED OWNERSHIP;

AUDITABLE CONSTITUTIONAL SPENDING AUTHORITY;

STATE AND LOCAL SPENDING ACCOUNTABILITY;

SINKING-FUND FINANCIAL DISCIPLINE;

NON-PERMANENT ASSUMPTIONS ABOUT TAX RATES;

AN END TO TAXPAYERS AS THE AUTOMATIC BACKSTOP FOR POOR PLANNING;

FOUR-MONTH NODE ACCOUNTABILITY;

CONTROLLED CONSEQUENCES FOR NODE FAILURE;

TRANSPARENCY OF COLLECTION AND EXPENDITURE;

EXPIRATION / REAUTHORIZATION WHERE REQUIRED;

and

ENFORCEABLE CONTROLS AGAINST GOVERNMENT DRIFT.

## 15. AUTHORITY AND EVIDENCE BOUNDARY

The preceding statements establish HUMAN ENGINEERING INTENT.

They do NOT establish:

current Kansas constitutional law;

current Kansas statutory authority;

the legal existence of continuing taxpayer ownership after taxes are paid;

the constitutional boundary of every state or local expenditure;

the legal or economic incidence of every existing tax;

the legality of any proposed taxable event;

the appropriate tax rate;

the amount of replacement revenue required;

the definition of a governmental node;

the legal consequence of node failure;

or the final Bill A architecture.

Those matters require:

CONTROLLED RESEARCH;

PRIMARY-SOURCE VERIFICATION;

LEGAL ANALYSIS;

FISCAL MODELING;

ENGINEERING DEFINITION;

and

HUMAN DISPOSITION.

## 16. WORKING ARCHITECTURAL CHAIN

The present Human Engineering Intent can be summarized as:

PERSON WITH ECONOMIC RESOURCES
→ PROPERTY RIGHT
→ CLEAR GOVERNMENT SIGNAL
→ AUTHORIZED TAXABLE EVENT
→ BOUNDED REVENUE CLAIM
→ COLLECTION / TRANSMISSION MECHANISM
→ GOVERNMENT POOLING
→ TAXPAYER-OWNERSHIP / STEWARDSHIP CONTROL
→ CONSTITUTIONALLY AUTHORIZED SPENDING
→ GOVERNMENT NODE
→ FOUR-MONTH ACCOUNTABILITY
→ PERFORMANCE SIGNAL
→ CONTINUED AUTHORITY / CORRECTIVE ACTION / FAILURE RESPONSE
→ EXPIRATION OR REAUTHORIZATION

The architecture remains under Engineering Definition.

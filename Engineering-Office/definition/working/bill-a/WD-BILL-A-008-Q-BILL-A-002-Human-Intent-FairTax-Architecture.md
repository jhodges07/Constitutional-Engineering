# BILL A — HUMAN ENGINEERING INTENT
WORKING DEFINITION — Q-BILL-A-002 FAIRTax ARCHITECTURE

## 1. GOVERNING MIRROR RULE

Bill A shall mirror the substantive economic tax architecture of the current H.R. 25 FairTax Act.

Do not create a separate Kansas definition where H.R. 25 already defines the transaction, property, service, exemption, business purpose, investment purpose, used property, mixed use, conversion, gaming transaction, or other economic classification necessary to determine FairTax treatment.

The Human intent is to make the Kansas FairTax system operate, to the maximum extent legally and operationally possible, as a state-level mirror of the H.R. 25 federal FairTax architecture.

## 2. PRIMARY RULE — EXACT OPERATIVE LANGUAGE

Use the exact operative H.R. 25 language wherever the intended Kansas rule is substantively identical.

Do not paraphrase H.R. 25 merely for stylistic purposes if paraphrasing could change the meaning, boundary, exemption, definition, calculation, or treatment.

Federal-specific references and machinery that cannot operate at the Kansas level shall be identified and translated only as necessary for Kansas jurisdiction and administration. Do not silently alter the underlying H.R. 25 economic standard.

## 3. INTENDED ARCHITECTURE COVERAGE (MINIMUM)

The intended architecture therefore includes the H.R. 25 treatment of, at minimum:

- taxable property and taxable services;
- final consumption;
- business-purpose purchases;
- investment-purpose purchases;
- used property;
- mixed-use property and services;
- business-use and personal-use conversions;
- gaming, chances, wagers, lotteries, casinos, horse racing, and related gaming services;
- exemptions, credits, registration requirements, documentation requirements, anti-avoidance provisions, and other mechanisms necessary to preserve the same economic boundary.

## 4. EXISTENCE IS NOT A TAXABLE EVENT

The mere existence, ownership, possession, accumulation, or appreciation of wealth shall not create a Kansas FairTax taxable event.

This includes, subject to later precise definition:

wealth;  
money;  
savings;  
income merely because it was earned or received;  
land;  
real property;  
personal property;  
investments;  
and other owned economic resources.

The permitted Kansas FairTax taxable-event architecture is final consumption as defined and controlled by H.R. 25.

## 5. USED PROPERTY

Use the H.R. 25 definition and treatment of used property.

Do not create an independent Kansas definition based merely upon whether an individual previously held legal title.

The purpose is to prevent repeated taxation of property that has already crossed the applicable H.R. 25 final-consumption/use boundary.

## 6. BUSINESS TRANSACTIONS

Use the H.R. 25 business-purpose standard exactly as the economic model.

A transaction does not become final personal consumption merely because ownership changes during production, distribution, resale, investment, or bona fide business use.

Use H.R. 25 rules for mixed business/personal use rather than inventing a separate Kansas allocation system.

## 7. GAMING AND LOTTERIES

Use the H.R. 25 gaming architecture.

Kansas Lottery tickets, casino wagers, horse-racing wagers, sports wagers, raffles, chances, gaming services, payouts, and related transactions shall receive the same Kansas FairTax classification that the corresponding transaction receives under H.R. 25, adapted only where necessary because Kansas rather than the federal government is administering the system.

Do not create a separate Kansas FairTax on a lottery ticket or wager if H.R. 25 does not treat the corresponding chance as taxable property or a taxable service.

## 8. KANSAS EXCISE-TAX EXCEPTION (EXPRESS DEVIATION)

The presently identified Kansas deviation concerns excise taxes expressly retained by Bill A.

Where an excise tax is retained for a specific product or activity, the Kansas FairTax shall not be stacked alongside that excise tax on the same transaction unless the Human Engineer later expressly authorizes otherwise.

Motor fuel is the current Human-defined example.

If Kansas retains a motor-fuel excise tax, gasoline or diesel subject to that excise tax shall not simultaneously incur the Kansas FairTax on the same purchase.

The retained motor-fuel excise tax applies to both personal and business purchases. A business-purpose purchase does not create an exemption from the retained motor-fuel excise tax merely because H.R. 25 would provide business treatment under the ordinary FairTax.

## 9. EXCISE-TAX PURPOSE CONTROL

Every excise tax surviving Bill A must have an expressly declared, bounded purpose.

An excise-tax pool is not general state revenue.

One hundred percent of the excise-tax proceeds must remain restricted to direct execution of the expressly declared purpose.

No state administrative charge, administrative overhead, management charge, allocation charge, accounting charge, fund-management charge, supervisory charge, or similar government administrative expense may be charged against the excise-tax pool merely because government administers the program.

The excise-tax pool shall not be used to pay state employees whose function is administration, management, supervision, accounting, allocation, contracting administration, or distribution of the pool.

Example:

If Kansas declares that a motor-fuel excise tax exists to build, repair, preserve, and maintain roads, the money must go to that declared road purpose.

The pool may pay direct road construction and repair costs and other expressly authorized direct-purpose expenditures.

If snow and ice removal is included in the declared road purpose, direct snow-removal operations may include snow-plow drivers, qualifying equipment, fuel, materials, and other direct operational costs.

The restricted pool shall not pay governmental management or administrative personnel merely because those employees manage the road program, contractors, snow-removal operation, or distribution of the money.

Engineering must establish a testable boundary between DIRECT PURPOSE EXECUTION and GOVERNMENT ADMINISTRATION / OVERHEAD.

The intended control chain is:

```text
EXCISE TAX
→ EXPRESS PURPOSE
→ RESTRICTED POOL
→ DIRECT PURPOSE EXECUTION
```

Not:

```text
EXCISE TAX
→ GOVERNMENT ADMINISTRATION
→ OVERHEAD
→ REMAINDER AVAILABLE FOR PURPOSE
```

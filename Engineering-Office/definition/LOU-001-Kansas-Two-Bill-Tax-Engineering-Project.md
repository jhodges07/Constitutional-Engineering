# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Letter of Understanding is to place in writing the objectives, intent, boundaries, current understanding, unresolved questions, and engineering direction of the Kansas Two-Bill Tax Engineering Project before formal requirements engineering or legislative drafting begins.

The project will engineer two independently controlled Kansas legislative products.

Both products share one fundamental destination:

**Eliminate Kansas property taxation through a controlled five-year transition, such that Kansas property taxation = zero at the end of that transition.**

The difference between the two products is not the property-tax destination. The difference is what each bill is responsible for legislatively engineering.

### Core Distinction

**Bill B** establishes the five-year property-tax elimination mandate.  
**Bill A** establishes the same mandate and also engineers the replacement tax / government-finance system.

### Bill B — Five-Year Kansas Property Tax Elimination Mandate

Bill B shall eliminate Kansas property taxation over a five-year period.

Bill B's primary purpose is to establish the legal and, where required, constitutional mandate, transition schedule, and enforceable destination under which Kansas property taxation reaches zero at the end of the five-year transition.

Bill B is **not** intended to engineer a comprehensive replacement Kansas tax system.

Bill B does **not** itself have to comprehensively solve or prescribe the resulting:

- state revenue architecture;
- local-government revenue architecture;
- school-finance replacement architecture;
- comprehensive tax-base replacement;
- comprehensive AGCL tax architecture; or
- other broader fiscal restructuring caused by property-tax elimination.

Those repercussions must be addressed through other legislation, governmental actions, appropriations, constitutional measures, transition measures, or other lawful mechanisms necessary to comply with the five-year property-tax elimination mandate.

Bill B therefore establishes the mandatory destination:

**KANSAS PROPERTY TAXATION = ZERO**

at the end of the five-year transition.

The existence of unresolved fiscal repercussions does not change Bill B's fundamental objective.

Bill B forces those repercussions to be addressed without making Bill B itself the comprehensive replacement-system legislation.

Bill B may ultimately contain whatever provisions are later determined necessary to establish, protect, sequence, enforce, or constitutionally enable the five-year elimination mandate. This LOU does not decide what those provisions will be and does not create requirements from that possibility.

### Bill A — Five-Year Property Tax Elimination Plus Comprehensive AGCL Replacement Architecture

Bill A shall establish the same five-year elimination of Kansas property taxation.

Its property-tax destination is the same as Bill B:

**KANSAS PROPERTY TAXATION = ZERO**

at the end of the five-year transition.

Bill A, however, shall go further.

Bill A shall engineer the replacement Kansas tax and government-finance architecture needed to accompany that elimination.

That replacement architecture shall be developed using the AGCL normative Control Documents as engineering/design constraints, subject to controlling Kansas and United States constitutional and legal authority.

Bill A therefore combines:

1. the same five-year property-tax elimination mandate; **and**
2. comprehensive tax and government-finance replacement/reengineering based on the AGCL normative Control Documents.

### Independent Products

The two products are related but separately controlled.

Neither bill shall automatically control, authorize, or determine the final architecture of the other.

# 2. Human Engineering Intent

The Human Engineer intends to establish whether Kansas can permanently eliminate recurring property taxation over a five-year transition through a clear legislative/constitutional mandate (Bill B), and whether Kansas can also engineer a comprehensive replacement tax and government-finance architecture for that elimination using the AGCL normative Control Documents (Bill A).

The Human Engineer intends that Engineering Definition may identify and research the fiscal, governmental, school-finance, debt, revenue, and implementation repercussions of property-tax elimination so that the Human Engineer and Legislature understand them.

**Understanding a repercussion is not the same as making Bill B responsible for legislatively engineering the comprehensive replacement system.**

The project is not merely an exercise in lowering property-tax rates.

The intended destination for both bills' property-tax objective is elimination.

The five-year period is intended as a transition to that destination, not as a temporary tax reduction program.

The engineering effort shall therefore distinguish between:

1. temporary property-tax relief;
2. phased property-tax reduction; and
3. permanent elimination of property taxation.

The project shall engineer toward the third objective for the property-tax destination of both bills.

The project shall also examine whether the permanent protection sought requires constitutional amendment in addition to statutory changes.

# 3. Scope — In

The Engineering Definition phase includes examination of material issues necessary to understand the two legislative products and the consequences of the shared five-year property-tax elimination destination.

### 3.1 Shared Engineering Definition Understanding (Both Bills)

For Engineering Definition understanding — without converting Bill B into comprehensive replacement-system legislation — in-scope subjects include:

- five-year property-tax phase-out architecture for the elimination mandate;
- existing Kansas property-tax structure;
- property-tax revenue by governmental entity and purpose;
- identification of replacement-revenue needs created by elimination;
- identification of local-government finance repercussions;
- identification of school-finance repercussions;
- existing governmental debt and pledged revenues;
- bonded indebtedness;
- transition risks and failure scenarios;
- taxpayer protections relevant to the elimination mandate;
- constitutional authority;
- constitutional amendments potentially required for permanence of elimination;
- taxpayer experience and Kansas resident testimony;
- governmental transparency and accountability relevant to the mandate;
- enforcement and remedies relevant to the elimination mandate;
- protection against circumvention of property-tax elimination;
- implementation sequencing of the five-year elimination; and
- measurable conditions for determining whether the elimination transition is reaching zero.

These subjects may be researched and understood so consequences are visible. Identifying a consequence does not assign Bill B the duty to comprehensively legislate the replacement system.

### 3.2 Bill B Legislative Scope

Bill B in-scope legislative engineering concerns establishing, protecting, sequencing, enforcing, and, where required, constitutionally enabling the five-year property-tax elimination mandate and its zero destination.

Bill B is not in-scope as the comprehensive replacement Kansas tax / government-finance system bill.

### 3.3 Bill A Legislative Scope

Bill A in-scope legislative engineering includes:

- the same five-year property-tax elimination mandate and zero destination as Bill B; **and**
- comprehensive engineering of the replacement Kansas tax and government-finance architecture needed to accompany that elimination;
- evaluation and design of that replacement architecture using AGCL Controls 00A through 00J as design constraints, subject to controlling constitutional and legal authority;
- broader Kansas tax-system revision associated with that replacement architecture.

# 7. Agreed Understanding — Candidate

Subject to Human Engineer HG-D1 acceptance, the current candidate understanding is:

### 7.1 Five-Year Destination

Both legislative products shall be engineered around permanent elimination of Kansas property taxation over a five-year transition, with Kansas property taxation = zero at the end of that transition.

### 7.2 Bill B / Bill A Legislative Responsibility

Bill B establishes the five-year property-tax elimination mandate and enforceable zero destination. It is not the comprehensive replacement-system bill.

Bill A establishes the same five-year elimination mandate and additionally engineers the replacement tax and government-finance architecture using AGCL normative Control Documents as design constraints, subject to controlling law.

Other legislation and lawful governmental action must address repercussions necessary to comply with Bill B's elimination mandate where those repercussions are not within Bill B's elimination-mandate scope.

### 7.3 Property Ownership

The project shall examine the principle that continued lawful ownership of property should not, by itself, remain a perpetual taxable condition capable of forcing continuing payments to government merely to retain ownership.

The exact legal and constitutional expression of this principle remains to be engineered.

### 7.4 Understanding Repercussions Is Not Bill B Replacement Engineering

Property-tax elimination creates governmental functions, obligations, debts, and revenue dependencies that must be understood.

Engineering Definition may identify and research those repercussions.

Bill B does not become the comprehensive replacement Kansas tax system merely because those repercussions exist.

Comprehensive replacement/reengineering of the Kansas tax and government-finance architecture is Bill A's additional legislative responsibility (and/or the responsibility of other separately authorized legislation), not Bill B's primary purpose.

### 7.5 Fiscal Reality Controls

No replacement architecture shall be accepted merely because it satisfies a philosophical or political objective.

Revenue capacity, expenditures, debt, economic behavior, transition effects, local-government requirements, school-finance requirements, uncertainty, and failure scenarios must be examined using traceable evidence — particularly for Bill A and for understanding compliance needs created by Bill B's mandate.

### 7.6 No Disguised Recreation

The project shall examine mechanisms necessary to prevent abolition of a tax in name while substantially recreating the same recurring burden on property ownership through another label or mechanism.

### 7.7 Local Government

Kansas property-tax elimination creates local-government finance repercussions.

Counties, municipalities, schools, and other affected taxing subdivisions must therefore be treated as part of the Engineering Definition problem set so consequences are understood.

That understanding does not convert Bill B into comprehensive local-government revenue-replacement legislation. Comprehensive local-government / school-finance replacement architecture belongs to Bill A and/or other legislation and lawful governmental action necessary to comply with the elimination mandate.

### 7.8 Existing Obligations

Existing lawful debt and contractual obligations must be identified and understood.

Property-tax elimination shall not be engineered by pretending existing obligations do not exist.

Addressing those obligations through lawful transition may require Bill A provisions and/or other legislation and governmental action. Bill B establishes the elimination mandate; it does not itself have to comprehensively prescribe the full debt-restructuring architecture.

### 7.9 Constitutional Permanence

The project shall identify where statutory authority is sufficient and where permanent reform requires amendment of the Kansas Constitution.

Statutory drafting shall not be used to conceal a constitutional conflict.

### 7.10 Transparency

Where a successor tax or charge is engineered (especially under Bill A), taxpayers should be able to understand what tax or charge exists, its authority, its purpose, its cost, its recipient, its duration, and the governmental activity it supports.

### 7.11 Public Participation

Kansas residents and affected parties may contribute testimony, experience, objections, alternatives, data, and failure scenarios.

Public engagement informs Engineering Definition.

Public comments, Facebook polls, advocacy campaigns, experts, testimony, AI conclusions, and popularity do not independently create project requirements.

# 8. AGCL Alignment

### Bill B

AGCL controls may continue to constrain the Engineering Definition and engineering process for Bill B where applicable.

Bill B is **not** required to contain a comprehensive AGCL-based replacement tax architecture.

### Bill A

Bill A shall engineer the broader replacement Kansas tax and government-finance architecture using the AGCL normative Control Documents as design constraints, subject at all times to Kansas and United States constitutional requirements and applicable law.

Bill A's replacement architecture shall be evaluated for alignment with AGCL Controls 00A through 00J under those controlling-law limits.

### Relevant AGCL Control Families

### 00A — Authority and Expiration

Governmental authority shall be examined for defined purpose, scope, duration, performance, exit conditions, constitutional basis, expiration, and reauthorization.

### 00B — Signal and Non-Interference

Fiscal, economic, audit, taxpayer, and performance signals shall be preserved and material adverse information shall not be suppressed merely because it challenges the preferred architecture.

### 00C — Property Rights and Pooling

The tax architecture shall be examined against property-rights principles, ownership, pooling, reversion, and the relationship between governmental claims and private property.

### 00D — Exit and Disengagement

The system shall examine meaningful exit and the degree to which taxpayers can avoid or terminate tax exposure without surrendering lawfully owned property.

### 00E — Debt and Intergenerational Liability

Governmental debt shall have identifiable purpose, payer, funding source, maturity, and extinction.

The successor system should not solve today's transition by creating uncontrolled liabilities for future generations.

### 00F — Uncertainty and Black-Swan Stress

The architecture shall identify assumptions, failure modes, stress conditions, detection mechanisms, containment strategies, and predetermined responses.

A five-year transition shall be engineered to degrade gracefully rather than depend upon a single optimistic forecast.

### 00G — Transparency and Public Ledger

Material governmental tax, revenue, debt, authority, cost, duration, and status information shall be designed for meaningful public visibility.

### 00H — Constitutional Compatibility

Every material exercise of governmental authority must ultimately be traceable to lawful constitutional authority.

Where AGCL principles and existing Kansas constitutional structures conflict, the conflict shall be surfaced rather than silently reconciled.

### 00I — Enforcement and Remedy

The project shall examine meaningful and constitutionally lawful enforcement and taxpayer remedies for violations of the final system.

AGCL remedies that may conflict with Kansas or federal law require legal compatibility analysis before adoption.

### 00J — Amendment and Version Control

The final architecture shall protect against silent system drift.

Material changes should be explicit, traceable, reviewable, versioned, and subject to appropriate constitutional and legislative procedures.

Historical versions and engineering decisions shall be preserved rather than rewritten.

# 9. Five-Year Transition Principle

The five-year transition is a core Human Engineering objective for both bills' property-tax destination.

Engineering shall determine how the elimination mandate can reduce property-tax reliance in controlled stages until property taxation reaches zero.

The SPEC phase shall later determine the measurable annual requirements, safeguards, transition mechanics, and acceptance criteria necessary to accomplish that elimination objective for each bill's authorized scope.

The LOU intentionally does not establish annual percentages or final implementation mechanics.

Engineering Definition may identify that the transition creates needs concerning:

- revenue replacement;
- local-government continuity;
- school-finance continuity;
- existing debt;
- contractual obligations;
- taxpayer impacts;
- economic conditions;
- administrative conversion;
- constitutional timing;
- fiscal reserves;
- stress conditions; and
- failure containment.

Identifying those needs does not make Bill B the comprehensive replacement-system legislation.

Bill B establishes the elimination mandate and zero destination.  
Bill A engineers the comprehensive replacement tax / government-finance architecture.  
Other legislation and lawful governmental action may also be required to address repercussions necessary to comply with the elimination mandate.

The five-year objective shall not be interpreted as authority to ignore fiscal or constitutional reality.

Instead, it establishes the engineering destination against which alternatives shall be evaluated.

# 13. Assumptions

The following are provisional assumptions requiring verification or later engineering:

1. Kansas property taxation can be reduced to zero over a five-year period if an adequate elimination mandate, and where required constitutional protection, can be engineered for Bill B, with repercussions addressed through Bill A and/or other legislation and lawful governmental action.

2. Property-tax elimination will create material changes and compliance needs for state and local governmental finance; those needs must be understood and addressed, but not necessarily by making Bill B the comprehensive replacement-system bill.

3. Constitutional amendment is likely to be necessary for permanent protection, but the exact amendment requirements remain to be established through primary legal research.

4. Existing debt and contractual obligations cannot simply be disregarded.

5. Replacement revenue mechanisms (primarily Bill A / other legislation) will materially affect taxpayers, governmental entities, economic behavior, and political feasibility.

6. Bill A's comprehensive AGCL-aligned replacement architecture may require changes extending substantially beyond tax rates and tax bases.

These assumptions are not established facts or accepted requirements.
